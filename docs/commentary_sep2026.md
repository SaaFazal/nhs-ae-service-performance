# A&E performance commentary: September 2026

**What changed.** 74.4% of A&E patients in England were seen within 4 hours in September 2026, down 0.7 points from 75.0% in August and 7.6 points below the 82% target for March 2027. It is also worse than September 2025 (75.1%). Patients waiting 12 hours or more from a decision to admit rose by 5,631 to 50,582, the highest level since February 2026. Only 7 of the 120 major A&E trusts met the 82% target; 97 were Red (under 78%).

**Why.** Demand went up. Attendances rose 4.3% in a month to 2,444,743, and were 134,521 higher than a year earlier. The fall was spread across the country: six of the seven regions got worse, and only the South East improved (up 0.3 points). This looks like system wide pressure rather than a problem at a few trusts. The same dip happened last September (down 0.8 points after the summer), so part of the change is seasonal.

**What to look at.**
1. **North West.** The biggest regional fall (down 1.5 points to 71.8%) and 4 of the 10 worst trusts, including East Cheshire, the lowest of all 120 major A&E trusts at 49.8%.
2. **Birmingham Women's and Children's.** Down 15.5 points in one month (93.7% to 78.2%). A drop this sharp should be checked with the trust first, to rule out a recording change before treating it as a real decline.
3. **Winter.** September is already below last year and 12 hour waits are rising. Reaching 82% by March 2027 needs a 7.6 point improvement while winter demand grows, so the trusts with the most 12 hour waits should be the focus: University Hospitals Birmingham (2,085), Royal Free London (2,031) and Shrewsbury and Telford (1,373, which is also second worst on 4 hours).
